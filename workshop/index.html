Indoors, laptops, headphones. No fieldwork today. Nothing to install: everything runs in a browser tab.

Three rules for the day

  • Only your own recordings. Nothing from a sample library.
  • Every transformation is a claim about the place. Be able to say what it claims.
  • When the result is more interesting than the original, ask what you lost.

First fifteen minutes

Arrange the folder before you touch anything

Like this

originals/ siteA/ three takes siteB/ three takes siteC/ three takes soundwalk/ empty for now

Site names become the names you type later, so pick ones you can spell under pressure. originals is the folder you never edit; soundwalk is the one you will fill with clips at the end of the first block.

No files with you?

There is a shared fallback pack — three sites, three takes each, recorded around campus. Use it and say so in your share-out.

Working from someone else's recordings is a legitimate position. It is just a different one.

WAV or MP3, either is fine. Leave the takes exactly as the recorder made them — long, unedited, untidy. You will cut them down at 0:45, and you will want to go back to the full take more than once.

Last twenty minutes of the block

Cut clips before you write a line of code

A browser tab is not a studio

Every file you import is decoded and held in memory. A three-minute stereo take is about 63 MB once decoded; a ten-second mono clip is under 2 MB.

You cannot point at anything

begin and end are fractions of the whole file. On a four-minute take, 0.001 is a quarter of a second — the bird is somewhere you cannot type. On an eight-second clip, 0.1 lands where you meant.

Loops click

An untrimmed edge is a step in the waveform, and a step is a click once per cycle. Ten milliseconds of fade at each end is the whole fix.

Six moves per clip, then import the folder

In audiomass.co

  1. Drag one take onto the page.
  2. Drag across the waveform to select the seconds you want.
  3. Crop to the selection.
  4. Fade in, then fade out — a sliver at each end, not a swell.
  5. Normalise, so your clips sit at the same level as each other.
  6. Export as WAV into the right site folder.

Nothing you do here touches the original. If a clip still clicks when Strudel loops it, the fade did not reach the edge — go back to step 4. It is always step 4.

What to aim for

Two to four clips per site, each between 2 and 12 seconds. One short event, one texture, one longer bed — all of them inside twelve seconds. Mono is fine and halves everything.

Twelve is the ceiling because the lecture instruments keep the first twelve seconds of whatever you load. One number to remember instead of two.

originals/ never edit these soundwalk/ import this one siteA/ 01_event.wav 02_texture.wav 03_bed.wav

Number the files. Strudel indexes them in alphabetical order, so 01_, 02_, 03_ is what makes siteA:0 mean the clip you think it means. Then import soundwalk — sounds, import-sounds, import sounds folder — and stop there. The next block starts with it already loaded.

02

Strudel, from zero

Seventy minutes, everyone following along

strudel.cc

A live-coding instrument that lives in a browser tab

Nothing to install

Open the page and you are running. No account, no admin rights. It caches itself, so it keeps working if the wifi drops.

You edit while it plays

The code is the instrument. Change a number, press update, and the next cycle is different. Nothing stops.

It thinks in cycles

Everything repeats on a loop of a fixed length. That is its strength, and the thing you will fight today.

Strudel is a JavaScript version of TidalCycles, the Haskell live-coding language. Free and open source under the AGPL, built by a community rather than a company.

Your first line

s("siteA")

Type it. Press Ctrl+Enter. You should hear one of your own recordings, looping once per cycle.

Now change siteA to siteB and press Ctrl+Enter again. Notice that the change waits for the cycle to come round. That waiting is the whole design.

Four clicks

Open sounds in the right panel, then import-sounds, then press import sounds folder, then choose soundwalk.

The naming rule

Each subfolder becomes one sound name. The files inside it are indexed from zero in alphabetical order, and you pick between them with siteA:0, siteA:1 and so on. This is why you numbered them.

Nothing is uploaded. The folder is read on your own machine and kept in the browser, which is also why a fresh tab may ask for it again.

Seven moves. That is the whole language you need.

Write thisIt does this
s("siteA")Plays a take from your siteA folder
s("siteA:0 siteA:2")Chooses which take — files are numbered from zero
.speed(0.25)Quarter speed, two octaves down. Where the texture lives
.begin(0.2).end(0.3)Only part of the file, as a fraction of its length — which is why you cut short clips
.loopAt(8)Stretches the whole recording across eight cycles
.chop(16)Cuts it into pieces and plays them in order
.slow(4) · .room(0.6) · .lpf(800)Slower · more space around it · darker

Everything chains onto everything. There is no order to memorise — add one move at a time and listen after each.

Build it in front of them, one line at a time

// 1 · a sound s("siteA")

// 2 · slower, deeper — now it is a place, not a clip s("siteA").slow(4).speed(0.25)

// 3 · give it room, take the top off s("siteA").slow(4).speed(0.25).room(0.6).lpf(900)

// 4 · two places at once, the comma stacks them s("siteA").loopAt(8).speed(0.5), s("siteC:1").chop(16).slow(2).room(0.4)

Four steps, four minutes, and they have a piece. Then hand it over and let them wreck it.

How far this goes

One trip. Eight machines. Eight different countries.

The source

Freeform — Simon Pyke — Audio Tourism: Vietnam and China, 2001.

Two months travelling with a MiniDisc recorder, collecting sounds and instruments. Eight tracks, every one built from what he carried home and, in his words, "sampled and reshaped through machines".

The reinterpretations

The same material, handed to eight other people.

Autechre · Bill Laswell · Jan Jelinek · Atom™ · Shudo · Mash'ta · Colongib · Tal. Eight tracks, no two of them agreeing about what those recordings were of.

Nobody added a field recording. They all subtracted differently.

simonpyke.bandcamp.com freeform1.bandcamp.com

Strudel wants everything to have a beat. Your recordings did not.

It is a pattern language built on repeating cycles. Left alone it will turn a marsh into a drum machine.

slow, loopAt and low speed values are how you resist. If your piece has a pulse you did not choose, slow it by a factor of four and listen again.

Track 2 · Sonify

The board cannot record sound, so it has to decide what counts

The Nano 33 BLE Sense has a microphone, plus temperature, humidity, pressure and light. It has no card slot and little memory, so it computes features and logs those — which is exactly how every acoustic sensor network works. They ship numbers, never sound.

Log at 1 HzWhy it is in this course
Level, in dBRelative, not SPL. Calibrate against a phone app and admit the guess
Temperature · humidityThe two variables that most govern how an odour travels — the smell half of the course lives here
LightCheap proxy for shade, time of day and enclosure

Then map a column onto speed, lpf or gain and listen to the day go past.

Track 2 · Sonify

Two numbers, and no others

PDM.begin(1, 16000)
PDM.begin(1, 41667)

Anything else and the library returns zero and gives you nothing. At 16 kHz your ceiling is 8 kHz: most birdsong harmonics, most insects and most sibilance do not exist. At 41 667 Hz they come back.

Run both at the same spot. Then choose one, and say who you left out.
