Digital Sound

A lecture and a workshop about what happens to a place when you record it — and what you are allowed to do to the recording afterwards.

The two sessions

Both run in a browser. Nothing to install, no account, and they keep working offline once the page has loaded — so bring them back to your own files long after the course is over.

Session 1 · two hours

Digital Sound Lab

Sampling, quantisation, aliasing, the spectrogram, A-weighting and the politics of a single number. Eight live instruments — four of them take your own field recordings.

Open the lecture Session 2 · four hours

What you do to a recording

Your own takes through the instruments, cut down to clips, then Strudel taught from zero. Ends with sixty seconds each, played to the room.

Open the workshop

Arrow keys move, I opens the index, N shows the notes for whoever is teaching. Both decks read on a phone too.

Tools for the workshop day

Two browser tabs, in this order. Neither needs an install, an account or admin rights on the machine.

AudioMass

Cut your long takes down to short clips: select, crop, fade in and out, normalise, export as WAV. Free and open source, runs entirely in the tab — nothing is uploaded anywhere.

audiomass.co

Strudel

The live-coding instrument the second half of the workshop is built on. A JavaScript version of TidalCycles; you edit the code while the sound keeps playing.

strudel.cc

Clip spec — two to four clips per site, each between 2 and 12 seconds. One short event, one texture, one longer bed. Mono is fine. Twelve seconds is the ceiling because the lecture instruments keep the first twelve seconds of anything you load.

Folder — keep originals/ untouched beside a soundwalk/ folder holding one subfolder per site. Number the clips 01_, 02_, 03_: Strudel indexes them alphabetically, so the numbers are what make siteA:0 mean the clip you meant.

Import — in Strudel: sounds → import-sounds → import sounds folder → choose soundwalk. The folder is read from your own disk and never uploaded.

audiomass.co strudel.cc Strudel's own tutorial

Before the workshop

Record one site, three microphone positions, a few minutes each — moved rather than re-aimed: down at the ground, at your waist, held up and away. Only your own material. Put the files on your laptop, not only in the cloud: there is no fieldwork on the day and no time to make good on a missing folder.

Fallback sound pack

If you arrive without recordings, use this — three sites, three takes each, recorded around campus. Working from someone else's material is a legitimate position; it is just a different one, so say so in your share-out.

sounds/

What you hand over

Ninety seconds, played to the room: which place this was, the one transformation that changed it most and what that claims about the place, and what you lost that you wish you had kept.

The third question is the assessment rehearsal

Read three

Deliberately only three — the course caps additional reading at 100 pages and you will need some of that budget later.

Shaw & Bowers, Ambulation · NIME 2020
Field recording treated as performance rather than documentation — the exact shift the workshop asks you to make.
Westerkamp, Linking Soundscape Composition and Acoustic Ecology · 2002
The standard argument for composing with recordings rather than merely presenting them, by someone who has done it for forty years.
Droumeva, Soundmapping as critical cartography · 2017
What a sound map does and what it hides. Open access, and the backbone of the middle of the lecture.

Listen to two

Not optional homework. This is the clearest answer to the question you will actually be anxious about on the day — what is this supposed to sound like when it is finished.

Freeform — Audio Tourism: Vietnam and China · 2001
Two months travelling with a MiniDisc recorder. Eight tracks, every one built from what Simon Pyke carried home. One person, one trip, one set of decisions.
The reinterpretations of it
The same recordings handed to Autechre, Bill Laswell, Jan Jelinek, Atom™ and four others. Nobody added a field recording — they all subtracted differently.
simonpyke.bandcamp.com freeform1.bandcamp.com

Listen to a source track and then its reinterpretation back to back. That pairing is the whole argument of the workshop.
